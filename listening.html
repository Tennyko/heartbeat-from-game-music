Heartbeat from Game Music

What if a game’s own soundtrack could follow the player’s heart rate? ARE gradually changes the music’s pace and sound, making room for a heartbeat. We started with a few examples for our poster and are building a listening library across different kinds of game music.

What we plan to test next

How smooth is the transition?

We plan to compare ARE with a simple crossfade, where the music fades down as the heartbeat fades in. Each listener would hear one version of each of six tracks. We’ll vary which version people hear for each track and ask them to rate how continuous the change sounds and how clearly they can hear the heartbeat.

Could it fit a quiet moment in a game?

We’d like to try a gradual transition in a rest area after an intense sequence. We could compare the same scene with and without the transition and ask players whether it helps them unwind or pulls their attention away.

Could it make play feel more immersive?

In a later game prototype, we’d like to compare a soundtrack that responds to the player’s heart rate with a preset version of the same soundtrack. We want to find out when the response makes the scene feel more involving and when the heartbeat becomes distracting.
